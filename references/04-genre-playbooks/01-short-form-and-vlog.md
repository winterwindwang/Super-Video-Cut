# Short-Form Video and Vlog Editing Playbook

> Category: Genre playbooks | Topic: Short-form video and vlog (vertical feed / life documentation / on-camera selling) | Compiled: 2026-11-10 | Applies to: Creators on Douyin (抖音), Kuaishou (快手), WeChat Channels (视频号), Xiaohongshu (小红书), Bilibili (哔哩哔哩), and YouTube Shorts / Reels

> **Numeric disclaimer**: Every platform parameter in this document — resolution, aspect ratio, duration, frame rate, bitrate, file size, loudness — is "subject to the platform's latest official documentation" and must be re-verified before publishing. Conflicting figures or rules of thumb are marked TO VERIFY.

---

## 1. Genre goals and evaluation metrics

**Goal**: In a feed where a thumb hovers over the screen and can swipe away at any moment, complete the chain "get tapped → stay → finish → engage or convert." Vlogs layer on a sense of companionship and emotional resonance.

| Metric | Meaning | Healthy reference (industry rule of thumb, TO VERIFY) | Where to diagnose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Click-through rate (CTR) | Share of impressions that open the cover | Benchmark against top performers in the same niche | Cover, first frame, title |
| 3-second retention | Share of viewers still watching at the 3-second mark | If completion rate is below 50%, check the first 3 seconds first | First sentence, first frame, first subtitle line |
| 5-second completion | Share who reach the 5-second mark | If low, the opening over-explains | Is the conclusion up front? |
| Completion rate | Share of full plays | 15–45 seconds makes a high completion rate easier to earn | Pacing across the whole piece |
| Engagement rate | Likes, comments, shares and saves / plays | Comment rate affects recommendation weight | Ending, points of controversy |
| Follow conversion / GPM | Follows per view / revenue per thousand views | The core metric for selling | CTA, product card |

**Four drop-off points on the retention curve** (locate them with creator-backend data):

| Position | Symptom | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| First 3 seconds | Cliff-edge drop; the video stalls in the first recommendation wave | Rebuild the first sentence, the first frame and the first subtitle line |
| Seconds 5–8 | Viewers leave before the point arrives | Lead with the conclusion; cut the self-introduction and setup |
| Mid-section | A large drop halfway through | Cut dead shots; add progress cues |
| Before the ending | A drop in the final seconds | Stop once the conclusion lands; don't keep begging for follows |

> Review principle: change one variable at a time and validate the next post against same-niche data. Don't go by feel.

**Vertical 9:16 safe area** (percentages are measured rules of thumb, TO VERIFY, subject to the platform's latest official documentation):

| Area | Share of frame | Elements that overlay it | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Top | ~12% | Avatar, username, search bar | Keep the main title out |
| Right | ~15% | Like, comment and share buttons | Leave white space at the right edge of subtitles |
| Bottom | ~18% | Description, buy button | Raise the subtitle baseline above 18% |
| Central clean zone | 70–80% (keep key visuals within the central 60%) | Relatively unobstructed | Put faces, products and main titles here |

> One 1080×1920 master covers Douyin, WeChat Channels, Kuaishou, Reels and Shorts. When converting landscape to vertical, prefer "crop to fill" or "blur fill" to avoid large black bars; cropping 16:9 to 9:16 discards about 68% of the width, so the subject must be centered.

## 3. Structure template (timeline tables, ready to apply)

**Template A: 30-second vertical on-camera delivery / product pitch** (second by second)

| Timeline | Task | Shot and pacing | Subtitle requirements |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:01 | First frame is the hook | 1 strong visual, no setup | Large title, 2–6 characters |
| 0:01–0:03 | Give a reason to keep watching | 2–4 cuts within the first 3 seconds, landing on hits | Suspense, benefit or conflict — pick one, and it must pay off |
| 0:03–0:08 | Conclusion first | 2–4 seconds per shot; cut the pleasantries | First line is the core conclusion |
| 0:08–0:22 | Develop the body | 1 information point every 3–5 seconds, with numbered cues | ≤2 lines per screen, 12–15 characters per line |
| 0:22–0:27 | Climax / payoff | Densest cutting; cuts land on hits | Enlarge key numbers |
| 0:27–0:30 | Close + CTA | One conclusion plus one action | Don't stack a long plea for follows |

**Template B: 60-second vlog**

| Timeline | Task | Beat |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:03 | Highlight teaser | 3 fast cuts + an emotional hook |
| 0:03–0:10 | Establish the scene | 2 medium shots + inserts, 3–5 seconds each |
| 0:10–0:40 | 2–3 small events | A turn every 5–8 seconds |
| 0:40–0:52 | Emotional peak | 1 slow-motion hold + a music swell |
| 0:52–1:00 | Resolution | 2–3 seconds of breathing room, one CTA |

**Hook templates** (ASCII straight quotes inside the code block):

```
Suspense: "Most people get the last step wrong"
Benefit: "Three methods that save you the detours"
Conflict: "Stop copying what those million-follower creators do"
Contrast: "399 full price, 99 today"
```

## 4. Key techniques and specific parameters

| Item | Parameter | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Cuts in the first 3 seconds | 2–4 | Fewer than 2 feels slow; more than 5 feels chaotic |
| Shot length in the first 3 seconds | 0.7–1.5 s | Locked to musical hits |
| Shot length in the body | 2–4 s for on-camera; 3–5 s for vlog | Don't hold one shot through a whole conversation |
| Information point interval | 1 every 3–5 s | Pull viewers back with "so what happened?" or "here's the kicker" |
| Beat math | 120 BPM = 0.5 s per beat; 4 beats = 2 s | Cutting in 0.2–0.4 s before the chorus locks the beat better |
| Dead air | Remove pauses >1.5 s | 1.2–1.3× overall, back to original speed on key moments |
| Subtitle density | 12–15 characters per line, ≤2 lines per screen, ≥0.8 s on screen | Readable at a glance in vertical |
| Subtitle position | Above the bottom 18% | Clear the 15% button column on the right |
| Cover text | ≤8 characters, 1 core message (TO VERIFY) | Match the expectation set by the first frame and title |
| Overall loudness | About −14 to −12 LUFS, True Peak ≤ −1 dBTP (TO VERIFY) | LRA 4–7 LU for on-camera delivery |
| BGM | Voice −6 dB / music −12 dB, or BGM −18 to −24 dB | Keep it below the voice |

> Beat-matching workflow: lay in the final music → drop markers on the BPM → snap cut points to the markers. Never cut on every beat; let picture rhythm play against music rhythm.

## 5. Audio and subtitle specifics

1. **Sound-on and sound-off, both covered**: Prepare readable subtitles for muted viewing and an audio hook for viewers with sound on. The test is to watch the whole piece muted.
2. **Vertical subtitles**: For feed platforms, burn in hard subtitles and enlarge key numbers with kinetic text. For educational content, also provide closed captions that can be switched off (see category 04).
3. **The loudness normalization trap**: Platforms pull back the program as a whole, which does nothing for balance inside it. At −14 LUFS overall, with the voice at −20 and the music at −10, the voice still sits too quiet after normalization. Balance within the program first, then make the whole thing compliant.
4. **Platform transcoding differences**: Douyin's transcode tends to boost saturation and contrast (TO VERIFY, subject to the platform's latest official documentation), so for Douyin consider dropping saturation slightly to leave headroom; WeChat Channels and Bilibili are more neutral. For one master distributed across platforms, grade to neutral and adjust the Douyin version separately.
5. **First frame and cover**: The first frame is the only image in vertical that must be designed on its own, and it has to carry the topic and the benefit immediately. The cover, the title and the first 3 seconds must promise the same thing, or viewers leave because their expectations were mismatched.

## 6. Common pitfalls and anti-patterns

| Pitfall | Anti-pattern | Consequence | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening pleasantries | "Hi everyone, today I want to share..." | Viewers drop in the first 3 seconds | Make the conclusion the first line |
| A hook that never pays off | Promising "three methods" and delivering one | Retention collapses mid-video | Match the promise to the content |
| Subtitles buried under UI | Subtitles pinned to the bottom, under the like column | Viewers can't read them | Lift them into the central clean zone |
| Shipping landscape as vertical | 16:9 with huge black bars top and bottom | The video occupies 1/3 of the height | Crop to fill or blur fill |
| Treating runtime cuts as the goal | Hacking 60 seconds down to 30 and losing the substance | Completion flat, information gone | Ask of every line: does cutting it hurt comprehension? |
| A dragging ending | "Like, follow, comment below" in one breath | Completion dips in the final seconds | Stop once the conclusion lands |
| Bitrate too high | Uploading 4K at extreme bitrate | Re-compression makes it softer | 1080P at a sensible bitrate |
| Cutting on every beat | A new shot on every drum hit | Visual fatigue | Long shots in verses, dense cutting in choruses |
| BGM over the voice | Music louder than the narration | Unusable when muted | Anchor on the voice; pull the BGM down |

## 7. Pre-delivery checklist

- [ ] The first 3 seconds give a clear reason to keep watching, and the rest delivers on it
- [ ] The first frame is designed on its own; the cover is ≤8 characters; cover, title and first 3 seconds promise the same thing
- [ ] The conclusion is up front; pleasantries and pauses >1.5 s are gone
- [ ] 1 information point every 3–5 seconds, with progress cues through the middle
- [ ] Cuts snap to music markers and the beat-cutting is not overdone
- [ ] Aspect ratio, resolution, frame rate and codec match the target platform (re-verified against the platform's latest official documentation)
- [ ] Key visuals, faces and the main title sit inside the central 60% clean zone
- [ ] Subtitles sit above the bottom 18%, at 12–15 characters per line, on screen ≥0.8 s, readable when muted
- [ ] Overall −14 to −12 LUFS at −1 dBTP, with voice and music already balanced inside the program
- [ ] The ending lands cleanly with a single-action CTA
- [ ] Real-device preview shows no re-compression, no A/V sync drift and no UI occlusion
- [ ] The backend retention curve has identified the biggest drop-off point, and the next post changes only one variable

# Chinese Copyright Law and Audiovisual Works

> Category: Copyright and compliance | Topic: Core provisions of the Copyright Law (third amendment, 2020), ownership of audiovisual works and infringement liability | Compiled: 2026-11-10 | Applies to: the baseline for determining ownership of source material, the bundle of rights, terms of protection and infringement liability

## 1. Core rules (article by article)

**1. Version.** The Copyright Law of the People's Republic of China (中华人民共和国著作权法) was adopted on 1990-09-07 and amended three times, in 2001, 2010 and **2020-11-11** (23rd Session of the Standing Committee of the 13th National People's Congress), and **entered into force on 2021-06-01**; "cinematographic works and works created by a process analogous to cinematography" was replaced by "**audiovisual works**". Always cite article numbers from the 2020 version.

**2. Article 3 and Article 5.** A work is "an intellectual achievement in the fields of literature, art and science that has **originality** and can be expressed in a certain form, including ... (6) **audiovisual works** ... (9) other intellectual achievements that conform to the characteristics of works". Mechanical clip-splitting does not produce a new work. Article 5 excludes laws and regulations and their official translations, **mere factual news**, and calendars, general numerical tables, general tables and formulas.

**3. Article 10 — the bundle of rights (17 items).** Relevant to editing: (5) the **right of reproduction**, "to make one or more copies of a work by printing, photocopying, rubbing, sound recording, video recording, duplicating, re-photographing, **digitising** or other means" (the 2020 amendment added "digitising"; importing, transcoding, frame extraction and exporting all constitute reproduction); (9) the right of performance; (11) the right of broadcasting; (12) the **right of communication through information networks**, "to make a work available to the public by wire or wireless means, so that the public may obtain the work at a time and place individually chosen by them" (the core controlled act for uploading to a platform); (13) the right of fixation; (14) the **right of adaptation**, "to change a work and create a new work with originality"; (16) the right of compilation. Among the moral rights, (4) the **right of integrity** constrains malicious editing and quotation out of context. **Article 29**: "Where a contract does not expressly license or assign a right, the other party **may not exercise** that right without the copyright owner's consent" — a licence must set out each type of right item by item.

**4. Article 17 — ownership of audiovisual works (three paragraphs, as enacted).** "The copyright in a **cinematographic work or a television drama work** within an audiovisual work shall be enjoyed by the **producer**, but the screenwriter, director, cinematographer, lyricist, composer and other authors shall enjoy the right of authorship and the right to obtain remuneration under the contract concluded with the producer. The ownership of copyright in audiovisual works other than those specified in the preceding paragraph **shall be agreed between the parties**; where there is no agreement or the agreement is unclear, **it shall be enjoyed by the producer**, but the authors shall enjoy the right of authorship and the right to obtain remuneration. The authors of **works such as the script and the music that can be used separately** within an audiovisual work shall have the right to exercise their copyright separately." → For clips from theatrical films, look to the production company; for short videos, look to the agreement; the images and the music may belong to different right holders.

**5. Articles 13, 16 and 19.** Article 13: copyright in a derivative work belongs to the person who created the derivative work, "but **the exercise of copyright shall not infringe the copyright in the original work**". Article 16: to use a derivative work to produce a sound recording or video recording, the permission of the copyright owner of that work **and** of **the copyright owner of the original work** must be obtained and remuneration paid (dual authorisation). Article 19: for a commissioned work, "where the contract contains no explicit agreement or no contract has been concluded, **the copyright belongs to the party accepting the commission**".

**6. Articles 22 and 23 — term of protection.** The rights of authorship, of modification and of integrity "**shall not be subject to any limitation**". For works of natural persons, the term is the life of the author plus 50 years after death; for works of legal persons, works created in the course of employment and **audiovisual works**: the right of publication runs for 50 years after the work is created, and the economic rights run for **50 years after first publication**; "where a work has not been published within 50 years after its creation, this Law shall no longer protect it".

**7. Article 24 — fair use and the three-step test (key).** The chapeau reads: "the work may be used without the copyright owner's permission and without payment of remuneration, **provided that the name or title of the author and the title of the work are indicated, and that the normal use of the work is not affected and the legitimate rights and interests of the copyright owner are not unreasonably prejudiced**". The article lists **13 items**: (1) use for personal study, research or appreciation; (2) **appropriate quotation** from a published work for the purpose of introducing or commenting on a work or explaining an issue; (3) unavoidable reproduction or quotation in reporting current events; (4) re-publication by the media of articles on political, economic or religious current affairs (unless a statement prohibiting use has been made); (5) speeches at public gatherings (unless a statement prohibiting use has been made); (6) limited reproduction for classroom teaching or scientific research, provided that the work is not published or distributed; (7) use by State organs in the performance of official duties; (8) reproduction by libraries and similar institutions of works in their own collections for display or preservation; (9) free performance (no admission fee charged, no remuneration paid and no profit-making purpose); (10) copying, painting, photographing or filming of an artistic work **in a public place**; (11) translation of works in the standard spoken and written Chinese language into minority languages and publication within China; (12) making works available to persons with print disabilities in accessible formats; (13) **other circumstances provided for by laws and administrative regulations**. Paragraph 2: "the provisions of the preceding paragraph apply to restrictions on rights related to copyright". Article 21 of the Implementing Regulations: "where a published work is used without the copyright owner's permission, **the normal use of the work shall not be affected and the legitimate rights and interests of the copyright owner shall not be unreasonably prejudiced**" — a two-gate test: fall within the enumerated item first, then pass the three-step test.

**8. Articles 25 and 49–51.** Article 25 is the statutory licence for textbooks (no permission required, but remuneration must be paid). Article 49 prohibits deliberately circumventing or destroying technological measures; Article 51: "without the permission of the right holder, no one may ... **deliberately delete or alter** ... rights management information ... and, knowing or having reasonable grounds to know [of the alteration], still make [the work] available to the public" → **removing watermarks or erasing copyright information is an independent ground of infringement**.

**9. Article 52 — civil liability (11 items).** Liability to cease the infringement, eliminate the effects, offer an apology and pay compensation arises for: publishing a work without permission; publishing a work of joint authorship as a work created solely by oneself; affixing one's name to a work one has not created; **distorting or mutilating** a work; **plagiarism**; "using a work by exhibition, **by the method of producing an audiovisual work**, or by adaptation, translation, annotation or other means"; failing to pay remuneration that should be paid; renting an audiovisual work, computer software, sound recording or video recording without permission; using a publisher's typographical design; broadcasting, transmitting or recording a live performance without the performer's permission; and other circumstances.

**10. Article 53 — administrative and criminal liability (8 items).** "Where an act of infringement simultaneously prejudices the public interest, **the copyright administration department** shall order cessation ... **where the amount of illegal business turnover is 50,000 yuan or more, a fine of not less than one time but not more than five times the amount of illegal business turnover may be imposed concurrently; where there is no illegal business turnover, or the amount is difficult to calculate or is less than 50,000 yuan, a fine of not more than 250,000 yuan may be imposed concurrently; where a crime is constituted, criminal liability shall be investigated in accordance with law**." Items relevant to editing: (1) **reproducing, distributing, performing, screening, broadcasting, compiling or communicating to the public through information networks** a work without permission; (3) reproducing, distributing or communicating to the public a performance without the performer's permission; (4) reproducing, distributing or communicating to the public a sound recording or video recording without the permission of the producer of the recording; (5) playing, reproducing or communicating to the public a radio or television programme [without permission]; (6) deliberately circumventing or destroying technological measures; (7) deleting or altering rights management information and still making the work available; (8) producing or selling a work with another person's signature affixed thereto.

**11. Article 54 — compensation.** Actual loss / unlawful gains → reference to royalties → **punitive damages of one to five times** where the infringement is intentional and the circumstances are serious → statutory damages of **not less than 500 yuan and not more than 5,000,000 yuan** at the court's discretion; in addition, reasonable expenses, rules on obstruction of proof and destruction of infringing copies.

**12. Related regulations.** Implementing Regulations of the Copyright Law (中华人民共和国著作权法实施条例) (promulgated 2002, amended 2011 and 2013): Article 5 defines sound recordings, video recordings, producers of sound recordings and performers; **Article 19** provides that "the name of the author and the title of the work shall be indicated"; **Article 21** is the three-step test; Article 26 sets out the scope of neighbouring rights. Regulation on the Protection of the Right of Communication through Information Networks (信息网络传播权保护条例) (State Council Order No. 468, in force 2006-07-01, amended 2013): Article 2 requires permission and remuneration; Article 6 lists 8 online fair-use exceptions; **Articles 14–17** govern notice-and-takedown and counter-notice; Articles 20–23 set out four categories of safe harbour.

## 2. What this means for editing

1. Downloading/importing/transcoding/frame extraction = right of reproduction; cutting into fragments and re-sequencing = rights of adaptation and compilation, and is constrained by the right of integrity; uploading = right of communication through information networks; removing watermarks = independent infringement under Article 51.
2. Ownership verification: for feature films and series, go to the production company; for short videos, check the agreement or the producer; for music, check all three sides — composition, recording and performance.
3. **Credit ≠ licence**: indicating the author and the title of the work is only a precondition of fair use and cannot replace a licence.
4. Commercial use (advertising, product promotion, traffic generation) requires written authorisation stating the types of right, territory, term, media and sublicensing.
5. For older material, calculate the 50-year term under Article 23; for sound recordings, apply Article 44 ("50 years after the recording was first made"); the chain of title and the asset register must be kept on file.

## 3. Risk tier table

| Tier | Conduct | Principal provisions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 🔴 Extremely high | Re-uploading a complete film, serialised clip-splitting, removing watermarks, cracking encryption | Article 10(5), (12); Articles 51 and 53 |
| 🔴 Extremely high | Using film/TV clips or popular music in commercial advertising without permission | Articles 53 and 54 (including punitive damages of 1–5 times) |
| 🟠 High | Round-ups, compilations and plot recaps quoting large amounts of core footage | Article 24(2) + three-step test |
| 🟡 Medium | Quoting without credit; commissioned material with no agreed ownership | Article 24 chapeau; Implementing Regulations Article 19; Article 19 |
| 🟢 Low | Material whose term has expired, public domain material, material with complete written authorisation | Articles 23, 26 and 29 |

## 4. Self-check checklist

- [ ] Article numbers from the 2020 version are used (in force 2021-06-01); the 2010 version is not cited by mistake
- [ ] For each item of source material, the right holder, source, licence, types of right, territory, term, commercial use and sublicensing are recorded
- [ ] Ownership is allocated under Article 17 between "cinematographic/television drama works" and "other audiovisual works"
- [ ] Derivative material has both the "upstream + original" authorisations; watermarks and rights management information have not been removed
- [ ] Where fair use is asserted, the specific item + the three-step test + indication of author and title have all been checked
- [ ] Commercial use does not rely on the personal-study or appropriate-quotation defence; commissioned productions have written ownership terms
- [ ] Terms of protection have been calculated for older material; chain of title, correspondence and asset register are on file

## 6. TO VERIFY and points of contention

1. The old label "12 circumstances" versus the **13 items** actually listed in Article 24 (including the catch-all item (13)): expert articles published by the National Copyright Administration in 2021 still use the old label; this document lists 13 items following the statutory text.
2. As to the commencement date **2021-06-01**, only the adoption date of 2020-11-11 has been verified on npc.gov.cn; Presidential Order No. 62 and the commencement date have not been verified against the primary promulgated text.
3. The Implementing Regulations remain the 2013 amendment, which cites the old article numbers of the 2010 version and does not correspond to the 2020 version; according to the CAVCA website, the National Copyright Administration has consulted on a **draft amendment for public comment**; the year, progress and whether it has taken effect are TO VERIFY.
4. The Regulation on the Protection of the Right of Communication through Information Networks (2006/2013) has not been updated following the 2020 amendment, and its 8 items in Article 6 are worded inconsistently with the 13 items in Article 24; how the two are reconciled is TO VERIFY.
5. The treaty basis for the first step of the three-step test (Article 9(2) of the Berne Convention, Article 13 of TRIPS, Article 10 of the WCT) has not been checked against the WIPO website.
6. How the fines in Article 53 interact with Article 36 of the Implementing Regulations (which still refers to "Article 48" and 250,000 yuan) is TO VERIFY.
7. The proposition that under Article 19 "the commissioning party holds only a right of use within the purpose of the commission" is prevailing practice commentary, not the text of the provision; the precise scope is TO VERIFY.

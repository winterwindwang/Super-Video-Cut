# Derivative Edits and the Boundaries of Fair Use

> ⚠️ This document is creative reference material and is not legal advice; statutes, regulations and judicial approaches change, so for commercial or high-risk projects consult a qualified lawyer and rely on the latest official text.

> Category: Copyright and compliance | Topic: Risk tiering of clip-splitting / re-uploading / remixing / commentary / round-ups / Reaction videos, the factors in assessing appropriate quotation, and a compliance decision tree | Compiled: 2026-11-10 | Applies to: project appraisal and pre-publication review of derivative short videos

---

## 1. Core rules (article by article)

**1. "Derivative edit" is not a statutory exemption.** The Copyright Law contains no such concept; it must be broken down into controlled acts: capturing footage = Article 10(5), the right of reproduction; re-sequencing and changing the plot = (14) the right of adaptation / (16) the right of compilation; distorting the original meaning = (4) the right of integrity; uploading = (12) the right of communication through information networks; re-editing derivative material = dual authorisation under Articles 13 and 16. Article 13 expressly provides that the exercise of copyright in a derivative work "**shall not infringe the copyright in the original work**" — "I added subtitles and a voice-over" does not automatically dispense with the need for a licence.

**2. Article 24(2) and the chapeau.** "Quoting **an appropriate portion** of another person's published work in one's own work for the purpose of introducing or commenting on that work or explaining an issue" may be done without permission and without payment, but the chapeau requires that "**the name or title of the author and the title of the work shall be indicated, and the normal use of the work shall not be affected, nor shall the legitimate rights and interests of the copyright owner be unreasonably prejudiced**". All three obligations are cumulative: the duty to indicate (Article 19 of the Implementing Regulations), not affecting normal use, and not unreasonably prejudicing interests. Paragraph 2: this limitation "applies to restrictions on the rights related to copyright".

**3. The three-step test.** Article 21 of the Implementing Regulations: "where a published work is used without the copyright owner's permission, **the normal use of the work shall not be affected, nor shall the legitimate interests of the copyright owner be unreasonably prejudiced**". In other words, two gates: fall within the specific item first, then independently pass the three-step test.

**4. Beijing High People's Court, Guidelines on the Trial of Copyright Infringement Cases (北京市高级人民法院侵害著作权案件审理指南)** (beijing.gov.cn; a local court trial guideline, not a judicial interpretation):
- **7.10**: "where a defendant, without permission, **provides a work to others through information networks** and raises the fair use defence that the use is 'for personal study, research or appreciation of another person's published work', **the defence shall not be upheld**." → Once uploaded to a public platform, the personal-appreciation defence essentially fails.
- **7.11 — four factors for appropriate quotation**: "(1) whether the quoted work has already been published; (2) whether the purpose of the quotation is to introduce or comment on a work or to explain an issue; (3) whether the proportion of the quoted content in the allegedly infringing work is appropriate; (4) whether the act of quotation affects the normal use of the quoted work or prejudices the legitimate interests of its right holder."
- **7.12**: "where, without permission and for profit-making purposes, a person translates or reproduces another person's published work in education and training offered to the general public ... [the defence] shall not be upheld." See also 7.1 (grounds of defence), 7.3 (limited expression), 7.4 (necessary scenes), 7.6 (public domain) and 7.9 (lawful authorisation).

**5. Position of the National Copyright Administration** (ncac.gov.cn, 2021 Conference on Internet Copyright Protection and Development, Professor Liu Wenjie): "**merely splitting a film or television drama into short videos of a few minutes without adding anything — this kind of 're-upload' that breaks the whole into parts cannot be called secondary creation, and is basically a copyright infringement that the law does not permit**"; "**editing a film or television drama so that the audience can appreciate the original work or fragments of it within a short time — such secondary creation will usually also require the permission of the right holder of the original work**". At the same time, he acknowledged that there is room for commentary, criticism, satirical quotation (for example, "rounding up continuity errors in films and television dramas") and fair use for the purposes of introduction, explaining an issue, news reporting and education, but "such introduction must not be mere wholesale reproduction or an abridgement of the original"; and concluded that "the mere existence of editing in a video is not enough to establish that infringement exists".

**6. Enforcement priorities of "Jianwang 2023"** (ncac.gov.cn, 2023-10-23): "focus on rectifying **the infringing conduct of deleting, trimming and splitting audiovisual works into short-video form, adapting them into compilations, and disseminating them through the network without authorisation**"; on the platform side, rectifying "**abusing the 'safe harbour' rule on the pretext of user uploads, disseminating infringing works on the pretext of fair use, and delaying performance of notice-and-takedown obligations on the pretext of self-devised procedures**".

**7. Beijing Municipal Intellectual Property Office, Typical Copyright Issues (Digital Economy)**: clip-splitting may infringe the **right of authorship, the right of reproduction, the right of modification, the right of integrity and the right of communication through information networks**; "**the proportion quoted does not necessarily constitute a fair use exemption**"; "conduct that substantially presents the main images, main plot and other content of an audiovisual work exceeds the limits necessary for introduction or comment and objectively **substitutes for the original work** does not constitute fair use".

**8. Publicly reported typical cases** (case numbers and details are taken from law-firm or media accounts; see "TO VERIFY"):
- **"Illustrated Film" case** (Youku v. Shenzhen Shushu Technology; first instance (2019)京0491民初663号, appeal **(2020)京73民终187号**, Beijing Intellectual Property Court): **382 frames** of the series were captured to make a picture collection with accompanying text; the defendant argued that it used only **0.5%** of the original and that this was promotional appropriate quotation. The holding: "**the test for reasonable quotation does not depend solely on the proportion quoted; it should depend on the reasonable need to introduce, comment or explain**"; the collection "covered the main plot and key images of the series at issue", caused a substantive impact, exceeded the necessary limits and constituted infringement. → **A small proportion ≠ fair use**; what matters is whether the core expression is covered and whether substitution results.
- **(2021)京73民终4060号**: the short videos were "mostly a condensation of the content and key highlights of the works at issue ... and could even replace the works at issue"; the defence failed. **(2021)沪0115民初25717号**: uploading dubbed clips constituted "communication to the public" rather than personal use. **(2017)京0105民初10028号**: the use of stills and posters from a television drama "did not conflict with the normal exploitation of the work and did not unreasonably prejudice the legitimate interests of the copyright owner", and was held to be fair use. → The outcome is highly case-specific.

**9. Summary of factors.** Drawing on 7.11, the three-step test and the position of the National Copyright Administration, all of the following must be assessed: ① whether the material quoted has been published; ② whether the purpose is introduction/comment/explanation (rather than letting viewers "watch the whole original"); ③ whether the amount quoted is the necessary minimum and whether it forms the main body or a substantial part of the new video; ④ whether the author and the title of the work are indicated; ⑤ whether it **substantially substitutes** for the original; ⑥ the **profit-making nature** (revenue sharing, advertising, product promotion, traffic generation and corporate promotion are usually treated as commercial use); ⑦ the **degree of creative transformation**. **There is no bright-line rule that "using under X seconds / X% is safe".**

## 2. Risk tier table for six forms of derivative edit

| Form | Principal risks | Tier | Compliance points |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Re-uploading a complete film / forwarding after watermark removal | Right of reproduction, right of communication through information networks, Article 51 | 🔴 Extremely high | Prohibited; take down unless licensed |
| Clip-splitting (breaking the whole into parts) | Rights of reproduction/compilation; a Jianwang enforcement priority | 🔴 Extremely high | Expressly characterised as infringement by the National Copyright Administration; licence required |
| "X-minute movie" plot recaps | Substantial substitution (Illustrated Film case) | 🔴 Extremely high | Covering the main plot and key images is almost inevitably infringing |
| Remixes / compilations / round-ups | Rights of reproduction and compilation; showing key highlights readily substitutes | 🟠 High | Quotation limited to what the argument requires; the original serves only as support |
| Film reviews / shot-by-shot analysis / continuity-error round-ups | Excessive quotation, missing credit, commercial use | 🟡 Medium (can drop to 🟢) | Closest to appropriate quotation; control the proportion, credit each item |
| Reaction videos | The original usually appears throughout; high substitution risk | 🟠 High | Obtain a licence or switch to very short fragments |
| Spoofs / absurdist remixes / parody | Right of adaptation, right of integrity, right of reputation | 🟠 High | Satire has room, but no vilification or distortion |

**Profit-making character is a risk multiplier**: where the video is used for advertising, product promotion, corporate promotion or account monetisation, "affecting normal use / unreasonably prejudicing interests" in the three-step test is very easily established.

---

## 3. What this means for editing

1. Treat clip-splitting, plot recaps and Reaction videos as "licence required" from the outset; do not count on a defence.
2. Register the purpose of each quoted fragment ("to explain issue XX") and its necessity; indicate the author and the title of the work in three places — end credits, description and on-screen.
3. **Substitution self-test**: "After watching my video, does the audience still need to watch the original?" The Illustrated Film case already confirmed that 0.5% can infringe.
4. "Source credited / will delete on request" satisfies only the duty to indicate and does not constitute a licence; removing watermarks actually aggravates the fault.
5. Commercial use always requires written authorisation stating the types of right, territory, term and sublicensing; keep the drafts of creation and the asset register.

## 4. Decision tree for derivative-edit compliance self-check

```
Q0 Is all material original / licensed / public domain / CC-compatible? Yes→Publish (retain the chain of title) No→Q1
Q1 Is there a written licence covering "reproduction + adaptation + communication through information networks + commercial use + territory + term + sublicensing"?
   Yes→Publish within the licensed scope (reservation of rights, Article 29) No→Q2
Q2 Does the use fall within a specific item of Article 24? No→[STOP] return to Q1 or change the material Yes→Q3
Q3 Is the purpose "introduction / comment / explanation" rather than letting viewers skim the original?
   No→[🔴 STOP] re-uploads, clip-splitting and plot recaps end here Yes→Q4
Q4 Is the quotation the necessary minimum, and does it not form the main body / a substantial part / the key highlights of the new video?
   No→[🔴 STOP] cut the quotation down or obtain a licence Yes→Q5
Q5 Are the author's name (or title) and the title of the work indicated? No→Remedy it, otherwise the defence fails Yes→Q6
Q6 Does it affect normal use / produce substantial substitution / unreasonably prejudice the right holder (three-step test)?
   Yes→[🔴 STOP] No→Q7
Q7 Is it profit-making (advertising, product promotion, traffic generation, revenue sharing, corporate promotion)?
   Yes→[🟠 Convert to a commercial licence] No→[🟡/🟢 Low risk] publish after conservative trimming + full evidence retention
```

**Any step answered "uncertain" is treated as "licence required".**

## 5. Self-check checklist

- [ ] The form has been identified and checked against the risk table; clip-splitting and plot recaps are not treated as exempt
- [ ] For each quoted fragment, a one-sentence reason can be given for "which specific issue this explains"
- [ ] No continuous set of the original's core plot, key images or climax passages is used; the commentary still stands with the quotation removed
- [ ] The substitution self-test has been done; the author and the title of the work are indicated where viewers can see them
- [ ] Watermarks have not been removed and rights management information has not been altered; commercial projects have written licences or use self-owned material
- [ ] Evidence has been recorded against the four factors in 7.11; drafts, sources, licences and correspondence are on file
- [ ] "I only used X seconds" is not relied on as the sole basis

## 7. TO VERIFY and points of contention

1. **Illustrated Film case**: the case numbers (2019)京0491民初663号 / (2020)京73民终187号, the parties, "382 frames" and "0.5%" all come from law-firm or media accounts; the original judgment has not been obtained and is TO VERIFY.
2. **(2021)京73民终4060号, (2021)沪0115民初25717号, (2017)京0105民初10028号** and the "Huluwa case concerning *Somewhere Only We Know*": all are reported second-hand; the case numbers, courts and whether the judgments are final are TO VERIFY.
3. The Beijing High Court Guidelines are a local court guideline, not a judicial interpretation; the year of issue/amendment and whether they have been superseded are TO VERIFY.
4. The old label "12 circumstances" versus the 13 items of Article 24 (including the catch-all item): formal citation should follow the statutory text.
5. "Degree of creative transformation / transformative use" is a US fair use concept and is not a statutory element under Chinese law; whether and how it should be taken into account lacks a uniform interpretation.
6. The boundaries of "profit-making" (whether revenue sharing, follower growth, traffic generation and platform subsidies always amount to commercial use) are assessed differently in different localities; 7.12 addresses only profit-making public training.
7. There is no dedicated judicial guidance on the Reaction format; the risk tier is inferred from general principles and is TO VERIFY.
8. Whether later Jianwang campaigns (2024/2025/2026) continue to list clip-splitting and adapted compilations as priorities has not been verified year by year.
9. Expert articles from the National Copyright Administration are academic or policy commentary, not normative documents, and cannot be cited directly as a basis for adjudication.

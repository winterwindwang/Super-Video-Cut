# Music, Sound Effects and Recording Licensing

> ⚠️ This document is creative reference material and is not legal advice; statutes, regulations and judicial approaches change, so for commercial or high-risk projects consult a qualified lawyer and rely on the latest official text.

> Category: Copyright and compliance | Topic: Layered music rights, collective management organisations, platform music-library limits, commercial licensing and CC licences | Compiled: 2026-11-10 | Applies to: music compliance for scoring, sound effects, cover versions, sampling, live streaming and cross-border release

## 1. Core rules (article by article)

**1. Layered rights.** A single piece of audio stacks: ① **copyright in the lyrics and music** (musical works, Article 3(3); Article 4(3) of the Implementing Regulations: "songs, symphonies and other works with or without words that can be sung or played"); ② **performer's rights** (Articles 39–41); ③ **phonogram producer's rights** (Articles 42–45); ④ music within an audiovisual work may be exercised separately (Article 17, paragraph 3). Article 16: to produce a sound recording or video recording of a derivative work, the permission of the copyright owner of that work and of **the copyright owner of the original work** must be obtained and remuneration paid (dual authorisation).

**2. Music provisions of the Copyright Law (2020 version), article by article.**
- **Article 38**: "To perform a work by using another person's work, the performer shall obtain the copyright owner's permission and pay remuneration. **Where a performance is organised, the organiser shall obtain the copyright owner's permission and pay remuneration**."
- **Article 39 — the six performer's rights**: to claim performership; to protect the image inherent in the performance from distortion; to authorise live broadcasting and public transmission of the performance; to authorise sound recording and video recording; to authorise the reproduction, distribution and **rental** of sound recordings and video recordings embodying the performance; to authorise communication of the performance to the public through information networks. Paragraph 2: "a licensee that uses a work in the manner specified in items (3) to (6) of the preceding paragraph **shall also obtain the copyright owner's permission and pay remuneration**." (The 2020 amendment added "rental" to item (5).)
- **Article 40 — performances in the course of employment** (new): for a performance given by a performer to complete the tasks of the performing entity, the performer enjoys the rights to claim performership and to protect the image inherent in the performance from distortion, and "the ownership of the other rights shall be agreed between the parties. Where there is no agreement or the agreement is unclear, **the rights in a performance in the course of employment shall be enjoyed by the performing entity**". **Article 41**: the rights in items (1) and (2) are not subject to any limitation of term; those in items (3) to (6) run for 50 years.
- **Article 42**: a producer using another person's work to produce a sound recording or video recording must obtain the copyright owner's permission and pay remuneration; paragraph 2 is a **statutory licence**: "where a producer of sound recordings uses a musical work that another person has lawfully recorded as a sound recording to produce a sound recording, **it may do so without the copyright owner's permission, but shall pay remuneration in accordance with regulations; where the copyright owner has declared that the work may not be used, it may not be used**" (this resolves only "re-recording"; it does not resolve "taking the original recording and putting it to video").
- **Article 43**: the producer "shall conclude a contract with the performer and pay remuneration". **Article 44**: the producer enjoys the right to authorise reproduction, distribution, rental and communication to the public through information networks, and to be remunerated; the term of protection is 50 years, expiring on 31 December of the 50th year **after the recording was first made**; paragraph 2: "a licensee that reproduces, distributes or communicates to the public through information networks a sound recording or video recording shall **obtain the permission of both the copyright owner and the performer** and pay remuneration".
- **Article 45** (new): "**where a sound recording is used for public transmission by wire or wireless means, or for public broadcast through technical equipment that transmits sound, remuneration shall be paid to the producer of the sound recording**". **Articles 46 and 48**: radio stations and television stations may broadcast published works without permission but must pay remuneration; a television station broadcasting another person's audiovisual work or video recording must obtain the permission of the corresponding right holder and pay remuneration.

**3. Chinese-law equivalents of "synchronisation licence" and similar terms.** Sync Licence and Master Use Licence **are not the names of statutory rights in China**; they are European and American industry terms. Scoring should be broken down into: the right of reproduction (Article 10(5)), the rights of fixation/adaptation ((13), (14)), the right of communication through information networks ((12)), the right of performance ((9)) and the right of broadcasting ((11)). The contract must state item by item that "synchronisation with images, reproduction in audiovisual products and communication through information networks are permitted"; obtaining only a "right of performance" is far from sufficient.

**4. Collective management organisations (MCSC / CAVCA).** Article 8 (2020 version): "a copyright collective management organisation established in accordance with law is a **non-profit legal person** and, once authorised, may ... assert rights in its own name"; where negotiation on tariff standards fails, either party may apply to the national copyright administration department for a ruling. **MCSC (Music Copyright Society of China)** administers the rights relating to musical works (lyrics and music), in particular the right of performance; **CAVCA (China Audio-Video Copyright Association)** administers the rights relating to music television works (MVs), in particular the right of screening. The Notice on Regulating the Order of the Copyright Market in the Karaoke Sector (关于规范卡拉OK领域版权市场秩序的通知) issued by the National Copyright Administration and the Ministry of Culture and Tourism (**国版发〔2021〕1 号**) applies, under Article 26 of the Regulations on Collective Administration of Copyright (著作权集体管理条例), a **"two-in-one" licence**: the rights of the two organisations are licensed, charged for and distributed between them by CAVCA alone, on the principle of "**licence first, use later**". The National Copyright Administration's answers to reporters confirm that **five** collective management organisations have been established in China. **Key caution**: collective management addresses performance and screening scenarios involving numerous right holders; **synchronous scoring of short videos and music for advertising are usually not covered by its standard blanket licences**, and must be licensed directly from the copyright agent, record company or commercial music library.

**5. The three limits of platform music libraries ("this platform only / non-commercial")** (as explained by courts and copyright bodies): ① "a short video made by a user **may only be used within that platform**; if it is moved to another platform for publication, it will still infringe the rights of the music right holder", and platform music must be used rather than audio of unknown provenance; ② "although most of the music provided by the platform has been licensed, this is **not absolute**", so the "copyright check" function should be used to confirm; ③ "short videos made by users are **limited to personal use** ... **commercial use still requires the user to obtain a commercial licence for the specific song**". In addition: once a video is made public, "whether the use is for personal or commercial purposes, it has already departed from the fair-use premise of 'personal study, research or appreciation'". Frequent complaints: popular songs outside the library, film and television OSTs, cover versions and instrumental covers, long background playback in live streams, and music extracted from other videos.

**6. Types of commercial licence.** Synchronisation (Sync — permission from the lyrics/music side to combine the work with images) and Master (permission from the phonogram producer for a specific recording) must be **obtained separately**; a subscription or buy-out from a commercial library (Production Music / Royalty-Free) must be checked for whether it covers commercial advertising, all platforms, perpetuity and sublicensing to clients; a commissioned original belongs, in the absence of a written agreement, to **the party accepting the commission** (Article 19); the statutory licence in Article 42, paragraph 2 covers only re-making a sound recording. **Six elements of a licence**: types of right, territory, term, media and platforms, whether commercial use/sublicensing is permitted, and credit requirements (Article 29: rights not expressly licensed may not be exercised).

**7. Sound effects and sampling.** Sound effects may constitute sound recordings or musical works, or may engage voice rights. **Sampling another person's recording, even a few seconds, pitch-shifted or sped up, still falls within the right of reproduction and the phonogram producer's rights** — "a few seconds doesn't count as infringement" has no legal basis. Check whether the stock site permits commercial use, adaptation, embedding in audiovisual works and redistribution together with the finished video. Low risk: self-recorded foley, synthesis, and licensed sound-effect libraries that expressly permit commercial use, with proof of purchase retained.

**8. Royalty-free ≠ copyright-free.** Royalty-Free means "after a one-off payment, no **continuing** royalties are payable per use or per view within the agreed scope"; it does **not** mean there is no copyright and does **not** mean the work may be used at will. Common restrictions: no commercial use; limits on medium/territory/term; no standalone distribution of the audio; no resale or relicensing; no use in political, adult or derogatory content.

**9. CC licences (Creative Commons official).** Four conditions: **BY (attribution, required by all CC licences)**, **SA (share-alike)**, **NC (non-commercial)** and **ND (no derivatives)**; six main licences (**version 4.0, published 2013-11**, recommended):

| Licence | Commercial use | Adaptation and sharing | Conditions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| CC BY 4.0 | ✅ | ✅ | Attribution (most permissive) |
| CC BY-SA 4.0 | ✅ | ✅ | Attribution + derivatives under the same licence |
| CC BY-ND 4.0 | ✅ | ❌ | Adapted versions may not be shared |
| CC BY-NC 4.0 | ❌ | ✅ | Attribution |
| CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 | ❌ | ✅ | Attribution + same licence |
| CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 | ❌ | ❌ | Download and share the original only |

There are also **CC0 1.0** (waiver of copyright, contribution to the public domain) and the **Public Domain Mark**. The official definition of NonCommercial is "not primarily intended for or directed towards commercial advantage or monetary compensation", and "**NC depends on the use, not on the status of the user**"; CC licences do not reduce limitations and exceptions such as fair use, and are irrevocable. **Two fatal points**: ① **ND** — trimming, changing speed, beat-syncing and remixing almost inevitably constitute a derivative work, so using ND material and then sharing it breaches the condition and terminates the licence; ② **SA** may require the entire derivative video to be released under the same licence, which conflicts with commercial use and platform exclusivity agreements.

## 2. What this means for editing

1. Distinguish between using a "musical work" and using a "specific sound recording": the former needs a licence for the lyrics and music, and the latter also needs a master licence.
2. Beat-syncing, tempo changes and excerpting must be expressly permitted in the licence as "may be edited, adapted and excerpted"; avoid ND material for CC assets.
3. Music from a platform library may be **published on that platform only**; multi-platform or overseas distribution requires a fresh licence.
4. Commercial work, advertising and product promotion must not rely on platform libraries; buy a commercial licence or commission an original.
5. A self-recorded cover version may rely on Article 42, paragraph 2 (remuneration must be paid, and no prohibition statement must have been made), but using the original backing track additionally requires a recording licence; live streaming and offline public playback engage the right of performance and the right to remuneration under Article 45; keep the source, licence text and order receipt for every audio track.

## 3. Risk tier table

| Tier | Conduct | Basis / consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 🔴 Extremely high | Using platform library music or a popular song directly in a commercial advertisement | The platform licence does not cover commercial use; compensation under Articles 53 and 54 |
| 🔴 Extremely high | Moving music from platform A's library to Bilibili, Xiaohongshu or WeChat Channels with the finished video | "This platform only" — crossing the line is infringement |
| 🔴 Extremely high | Film and television OSTs, extracting audio from another person's video, long playback of popular songs in a live stream | No licence + unknown provenance |
| 🟠 High | Using "royalty-free/free" music without reading the terms; editing and adapting CC BY-ND material | Royalty-free has scope limits; breach of ND terminates the licence |
| 🟠 High | Sampling another person's recording for a few seconds, or pitch-shifting it | Right of reproduction + phonogram producer's rights; there is no "few-seconds safe line" |
| 🟡 Medium | Publishing a cover version; exceeding the medium/territory/term of a commercial library | Article 42, paragraph 2; reservation of rights under Article 29 |
| 🟢 Low | Self-recorded or synthesised sound effects, commissioned originals bought out in writing, CC0/public domain, full Sync + Master licence | Evidence must still be retained |

## 4. Self-check checklist

- [ ] Licences for the lyrics/music and the master have each been obtained; the licence covers reproduction/synchronisation/adaptation/online communication, commercial use, territory, term, platform and sublicensing
- [ ] Platform library music is published on that platform only and has been verified with the "copyright check" function; commercial projects do not rely on platform libraries
- [ ] The full licence terms of "royalty-free" music have been read, not merely the label
- [ ] CC material has been confirmed as version 4.0 and its type identified; **no ND material has been edited or adapted**, and SA contagion has been assessed
- [ ] Attribution has been given as required by the licence (author, title of the work, name of the licence and link)
- [ ] Sound effects and samples have been confirmed as usable commercially, adaptable and embeddable in audiovisual works
- [ ] Cover versions have been checked against the conditions of Article 42, paragraph 2; commissioned work has written ownership terms
- [ ] The music asset register, orders and licence texts are on file; territorial licensing has been considered for cross-border release

## 6. TO VERIFY and points of contention

1. **"Synchronisation right / Sync / Master Use Licence"** are not the names of statutory rights in China; their mapping to the Chinese list of rights is a doctrinal and practical construct.
2. The **three limits of platform music libraries** come from second-hand accounts by courts and copyright bodies; **the latest official agreements and library terms of Douyin, Kuaishou, Bilibili and WeChat Channels have not been checked item by item**.
3. **"A bank's short-video background-music infringement case"**: mentioned in the media, but with no case number and no full name of the adjudicating court.
4. **The application of collective management to short videos and live streaming**: the verified "two-in-one" arrangement is limited to karaoke; the CAVCA website carries a "work briefing on the right to remuneration for sound recordings in live-streaming rooms", but whether blanket licensing is available and what the tariff is are TO VERIFY.
5. **The current version of the Regulations on Collective Administration of Copyright**: the version cited is the 2013 amendment; whether it has been revised following the 2020 amendment of the Law, and whether the numbering of Article 26 has changed, are TO VERIFY.
6. **The complete list of China's "five collective management organisations"**: only the number and the two organisations MCSC and CAVCA have been confirmed; the other three are TO VERIFY.
7. **The legal characterisation of "royalty-free"**: it is a contractual licensing model; the complete agreement of any specific commercial music library has not been checked.
8. **The effect of CC licences before Chinese courts and the finding of NC**: how Chinese courts treat product promotion and revenue sharing as "commercial use" is TO VERIFY.
9. **The boundaries of the statutory licence in Article 42, paragraph 2**: what counts as "lawfully recorded", the form of a prohibition statement and the remuneration standard have not been verified against the competent department's rules.
10. **The tariff and collection/distribution mechanism for the right to remuneration for public broadcast of sound recordings under Article 45** are TO VERIFY.

## 1. Core rules (article by article)

**1. Right of portrait under the Civil Code (中华人民共和国民法典) (Part on Personality Rights, Chapter 4).**
- **Article 1018**: a portrait is "the **externally identifiable image** of a specific natural person reflected on a certain medium by means of images, sculpture, painting or other methods" → the test is **identifiability**: a profile, silhouette or partial feature that can point to a specific person may constitute a portrait.
- **Article 1019** (two paragraphs, key text): "No organisation or individual may infringe another person's right of portrait by vilification, defacement, **fabrication by information technology** or other means. **Without the consent of the person enjoying the right of portrait, no one may make, use or publish the portrait of that person, except as otherwise provided by law**"; "without the consent of the person enjoying the right of portrait, **the owner of the rights in a portrait work** may not use or publish the portrait of that person by publication, reproduction, distribution, rental, exhibition or other means." → Paragraph 1 brings **AI face-swapping and deepfakes** within the prohibition; paragraph 2 means that **even where the editor owns the copyright in the photograph or video, the portrait may not be published without the consent of the person depicted** (the two rights operate in parallel).
- **Article 1020** — five cases of fair use: use of an **already disclosed** portrait within the necessary scope for personal study, artistic appreciation, classroom teaching or scientific research; unavoidable use in news reporting; performance of official duties by State organs in accordance with law; unavoidable use in displaying a specific public environment; safeguarding the public interest or the lawful rights and interests of the person enjoying the right of portrait. → Commercial advertising and product promotion essentially never fall within these.
- **Article 1021**: where the terms are disputed, "an interpretation **in favour of the person enjoying the right of portrait** shall be adopted". **Article 1022**: where the term of the licence is not agreed or is unclear, either party may **terminate at any time**; where the term is clear, the person enjoying the right of portrait may also terminate for justified reasons.
- **Article 1023, paragraph 2** (text): "**the provisions on the protection of the right of portrait shall apply by reference to the protection of a natural person's voice**." → Voice is **a personality interest protected by reference to the right of portrait**.
- Related provisions: Article 995 (claims such as an apology **are not subject to the limitation of action**), Article 1182 (compensation for pecuniary loss involving personality rights), Article 1024 (right of reputation). Spoofs, absurdist remixes and malicious captions may also infringe the right of reputation.

**2. Privacy and personal information.**
- **Article 1032** (text): "Privacy is the **peace of private life** of a natural person and the **private space, private activities and private information** that the person does not wish others to know", and infringement by prying, intrusion, disclosure, publication or other means is prohibited (npc.gov.cn: the first time "peace of private life" was written into law).
- **Article 1033**: prohibits six categories of conduct, including "entering, **photographing** or peeping into another person's residence, hotel room or other private space", "**photographing, peeping at, eavesdropping on or publicising another person's private activities**", "photographing or peeping at another person's private parts" and "processing another person's private information". → Street photography, following shots, covert filming, and publicising arguments or medical treatment are extremely high risk.
- **Article 1034**: personal information includes name, date of birth, identity document number, **biometric information**, address, telephone number, email address, health information, **location information** and so on; "private information within personal information is governed by the provisions on the right of privacy". **Article 1035**: processing shall be lawful, justified, necessary and not excessive, and requires ① the consent of the natural person **or of their guardian**, ② publicised processing rules, ③ express statement of the purpose, method and scope, ④ no violation of laws or agreements. **Article 1036** lists three exemptions.
- **Personal Information Protection Law (中华人民共和国个人信息保护法)**: Article 4 ("processing" includes collection, storage, use, processing, transmission, provision, disclosure and deletion); **Article 13** — seven bases of lawfulness (consent; necessity for concluding or performing a contract or for human resources management; performance of statutory duties and obligations; response to a public health emergency or other emergency; news reporting and public opinion supervision in the public interest within a reasonable scope; reasonable processing of lawfully disclosed information; other circumstances provided by laws and administrative regulations); **Article 25** — "may not disclose the personal information it processes, **except with the separate consent of the individual**"; **Article 26** — image collection and identity recognition equipment in public places must be necessary for safeguarding public security and be marked with prominent notices, and "the personal images and identity recognition information collected may only be used for the purpose of safeguarding public security"; **Article 27** — reasonable processing of already disclosed personal information (except where the individual expressly refuses, and consent is required where the processing has a major impact on the individual's rights and interests); **Article 28** — sensitive personal information includes biometric information, religious belief, specific identity, medical health, financial accounts, whereabouts "**and the personal information of minors under 14 years of age**"; **Article 29** — "to process sensitive personal information, the **separate consent** of the individual shall be obtained"; **Article 31** — "to process the personal information of a minor under 14 years of age, the consent of the minor's **parents or other guardians** shall be obtained ... **special rules for the processing of personal information** shall be formulated".

**3. Minors on camera.** Information of a child under 14 is **sensitive personal information** and requires written guardian consent + separate consent + special processing rules + data minimisation; the licence should state the platforms, territory, term, whether commercial use is permitted and whether re-editing is permitted; avoid exposing names, schools, addresses and whereabouts, and pixelate or alter the voice where necessary.

**4. Font copyright and disputes over commercial licensing.** Official note of the Beijing Municipal Intellectual Property Office: "**the mainstream view in judicial practice is that, provided the originality requirement is satisfied, individual characters in a font library are works of fine art under the Copyright Law and are protected**", but "**a font library typeface is an intellectual achievement in which the artistic and the instrumental are closely combined ... its originality must meet a relatively high distinctive aesthetic requirement** ... **whether an individual character output by running the font software is original must be determined one by one**"; the library as a whole may also constitute **computer software copyright**. Typical Copyright Issues (Digital Economy): adhere to the principles of "**licence first, use later**" and "**no licence, no use**".
- **"Nine-Storey Demon Tower" font case** (media account, TO VERIFY): seven brush-calligraphy characters by Xiang Jiahong were used in a film prop; the first instance held that the individual characters "manifest a distinctive artistic aesthetic and are works of fine art", that this was not appropriate quotation, and that the right of reproduction and the right of authorship were infringed, ordering joint compensation of **140,000 yuan**; **the Beijing Intellectual Property Court upheld the judgment on appeal in November 2019**. The font was free to download but stated that "commercial use requires authorisation".
- **Jingdezhen Zhushan District Court font case** (reproduced by The Paper): 12 individual characters of font W were used on a Taobao product page, with damages of 3,500 yuan.
- Practice: prefer public-domain fonts, open-source fonts (noting the OFL and similar licences), or purchase a licence that expressly covers "video/film and television/broadcast/commercial use/delivery to clients", checking weight, glyph, medium, territory, term and sublicensing item by item.

**5. Key licence terms of stock image and asset sites.** Beijing Municipal Intellectual Property Office: "where there are people in an image, **dual authorisation** is required ... for a model photograph, copyright authorisation must be obtained from the copyright owner and **authorisation of the right of portrait from the model**"; for image libraries, note "1. **limits on the scope of the licence, for example whether adaptation rights or sublicensing rights are included**; 2. whether the **chain of authorisation of the image library is complete**". Must check: ① the licensor and the chain of title; ② the scope of use (medium, territory, term, commercial use, adaptation, sublicensing to clients, redistribution as a template); ③ **Model Release / Property Release**; ④ whether AI training and sensitive subject matter are permitted; ⑤ the cap on indemnity, takedown obligations and governing law. Platform "licensed image insert" features exist "but the restrictions on use must still be observed".

**6. Rights and risks in AI-generated content.**
- **Beijing Internet Court, "China's first 'AI text-to-image' copyright infringement case"** (the "Spring Breeze Brings Tenderness" case, court website): the plaintiff set prompts and parameters in Stable Diffusion and repeatedly screened the outputs; the defendant removed the watermark and used the image as an illustration. The court held that the image "reflects the plaintiff's **original intellectual input**" and is a protected work of fine art; it further stated that "**the Copyright Law provides that authors are limited to natural persons, legal persons or unincorporated organisations, so an AI model itself cannot become an author under China's Copyright Law**"; the plaintiff was "the person who made the relevant settings to the AI model at issue as required and ultimately selected the image at issue", and was the author enjoying copyright. The court ordered an apology and **500 yuan** in compensation; the first-instance judgment took effect; the case was selected among the ten most influential events in the development of China's digital economy and the rule of law in 2024 and was included in the work report of the Beijing High People's Court.
- **Contrary case**: the "Phantom Wing transparent chair" case (Suzhou Intermediate People's Court, 2025-04, as reported): the plaintiff could not reproduce the creative process and had no complete record of creation, and the court held that "**content generated by merely triggering AI through the input of simple prompts does not reflect original intellectual input by a human and does not constitute a work within the meaning of the Copyright Law**". → The dividing line is **whether the human intellectual input reaches the originality threshold**.
- **China's first "AI voice infringement case"** (Beijing Internet Court, first instance 2024-04, China Court Network): voice rights extend to **AI-synthesised voices** — "as long as the general public, or the public within a certain range, can identify a specific natural person by timbre, intonation and pronunciation style, that natural person's voice rights may extend to the AI voice"; using, or authorising another to use, the voice in a sound recording without permission constitutes infringement. The China Court Network article "Protecting voice rights in accordance with law and promoting the 'benign' development of artificial intelligence" cites Articles 1018, 1019 and 1023.
- Guangzhou Internet Court "Ultraman case" (as reported): the generated images were substantially similar to pre-existing characters → AI outputs **may also infringe pre-existing copyright**.
- Four categories of risk: ① the output may not be recognised as a work, so rights cannot be asserted and enforcement is difficult; ② training data or outputs infringe (substantial similarity, trademarks, likeness, reputation); ③ platform terms on ownership of outputs and commercial restrictions; ④ compliance obligations for generative AI services. Responses: keep records of the whole process, avoid specifying pre-existing works, real people or brands, make substantive human modifications, and run an infringement search before commercial use.

**7. Trademarks and brand exposure.** Logos, packaging, shopfront signs and product appearance may engage trademark rights, copyright and the law against unfair competition. Points to consider: whether there is "trademark use", whether it is incidental or descriptive fair use, whether it disparages or creates a false association, and whether the title or thumbnail implies brand endorsement. Commercial collaborations require written authorisation; pixelate or blur non-essential brands. **The specific provisions of the Trademark Law (中华人民共和国商标法) were not verified for this edition.**

## 2. What this means for editing

1. Every identifiable natural person requires a written portrait licence; copyright in a photograph or video is separate from the right of portrait, and **dual authorisation** is required.
2. Licence terms must be extremely explicit (Article 1021: disputes are interpreted in favour of the person enjoying the right of portrait) and must cover platforms, territory, term, commercial use and re-editing.
3. Do not film or disclose private space, private activities or private information; do not use AI face-swapping, voice imitation or voice alteration to impersonate a specific natural person.
4. Check the originality of individual characters and the commercial scope for fonts; check the chain of title and the Model/Property Release for stock images; keep full records of AI assets and make substantive human modifications.
5. Obtain authorisation for brand exposure or pixelate it; obtain written guardian consent plus separate consent for minors; assess the right of reputation for spoof content; file all authorisation documents.

## 3. Risk tier table

| Tier | Conduct | Principal basis / consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 🔴 Extremely high | AI face-swapping / deepfake portraits; cloning a voice for commercial use | Articles 1019 and 1023; cessation of infringement + apology + compensation |
| 🔴 Extremely high | Covert filming / following shots / publicising private activities; disclosing identifiable images of individuals | Articles 1032 and 1033; PIPL Articles 25/26 |
| 🔴 Extremely high | Using another person's portrait in commercial advertising without authorisation (including passers-by in one's own footage) | Article 1019, paragraph 2: owning copyright ≠ holding the right of portrait |
| 🟠 High | Using unlicensed commercial fonts or stock images in commercial videos | Font cases (precedent of 140,000 yuan in damages); dual authorisation |
| 🟠 High | Minors on camera without written guardian consent; treating a simple-prompt AI image as own copyright for commercial use | PIPL Articles 28/29/31; Phantom Wing case |
| 🟡 Medium | Brand logos not pixelated; titles implying a collaboration | Trademark / unfair competition (article numbers TO VERIFY) |
| 🟢 Low | Own footage + complete written authorisation + public-domain or open-source fonts + no third-party brands | The chain of authorisation must still be retained |

## 4. Self-check checklist

- [ ] Every identifiable natural person has a written portrait licence covering platforms/territory/term/commercial use/re-editing
- [ ] Where copyright and the right of portrait are separate, dual authorisation has been obtained; no private space, activities or information has been filmed or disclosed
- [ ] No AI face-swapping, voice imitation or voice alteration has been used to impersonate a specific natural person
- [ ] Fonts have been checked for per-character licensing and commercial scope; stock images have been checked for the chain of title, adaptation rights and Model/Property Release
- [ ] AI assets have a complete record of creation and do not specify pre-existing works, real people or brands
- [ ] Brand exposure has been authorised or pixelated; for minors, written guardian consent plus separate consent has been obtained
- [ ] Spoof content has been assessed for the right of reputation; authorisation documents, drafts and registers are on file

## 6. TO VERIFY and points of contention

1. The application of **Article 1020** to short-video and commercial scenarios lacks dedicated judicial interpretation; how "unavoidable" and "necessary scope" are to be determined is TO VERIFY.
2. **The *Nine-Storey Demon Tower* font case**: the seven characters, the 140,000 yuan and the November 2019 affirmance on appeal all come from media accounts; no case number or original judgment has been seen.
3. **The Jingdezhen font case** (12 characters, 3,500 yuan): reproduced from court-based public legal education; the case number and whether the judgment is final are TO VERIFY.
4. **Whether an individual character constitutes a work of fine art**: only the formulation "mainstream view in judicial practice" is available, and academic opinion and courts remain divided; how software protection for the library and protection of individual characters fit together is TO VERIFY.
5. **The case numbers in the AI text-to-image cases**: not listed on the court website (the commonly cited (2023)京0491民初11279号 has not been officially confirmed); the names of the two cases, and the case number and final status of the Suzhou Intermediate People's Court judgment of 2025-04, all come from second-hand accounts.
6. **China's first "AI voice infringement case"**: the case number, the parties and the amount awarded have not been verified; the report is an explanation of a typical case.
7. **The Guangzhou "Ultraman case"**: reported second-hand only; the case number and the holding are TO VERIFY.
8. **Dedicated AI regulation**: the relevant provisions of the Interim Measures for the Administration of Generative Artificial Intelligence Services (生成式人工智能服务管理暂行办法) and the Provisions on the Administration of Deep Synthesis of Internet Information Services (互联网信息服务深度合成管理规定) were not verified for this edition.
9. **Provisions of the Trademark Law**: Article 48, Article 57 and the standards for "trademark use" and "incidental exposure" were not specifically verified.
10. **Special statutory restrictions on minors appearing on camera**: the article numbers and application of the Law on the Protection of Minors (未成年人保护法) and the Advertising Law (广告法) concerning advertising endorsements have not been verified.
11. **Article 13 of the PIPL**: the article number and the seven items come from second-hand accounts and were not checked against the primary text on npc.gov.cn or cac.gov.cn.
12. **Whether publishing another person's portrait also constitutes processing of personal information, and the concurrence of claims**: no dedicated judicial interpretation exists.
13. **Whether a finished video already published must be taken down after a licence under Article 1022 is terminated, and what transition period applies**, is TO VERIFY.

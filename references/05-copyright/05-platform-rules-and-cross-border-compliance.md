## 1. Core rules (article by article)

**1. Domestic platform rules and music libraries.** The user agreements of Douyin, Kuaishou, Bilibili, WeChat Channels and others generally require uploaded material to be **original or lawfully licensed**. The three limits of platform music libraries (see file 03): ① the licence usually **covers publication on that platform only**; moving the video to Bilibili, Xiaohongshu or WeChat Official Accounts may still infringe; ② "although most of the music provided by the platform has been licensed, this is **not absolute**", so use the "copyright check" function to confirm; ③ **personal/non-commercial only**: commercial advertising and corporate promotion require the user to obtain a licence independently. The National Copyright Administration's "Jianwang 2023" expressly "focuses on rectifying **the infringing conduct of deleting, trimming and splitting audiovisual works into short-video form, adapting them into compilations and disseminating them through the network without authorisation**", and on rectifying platforms that "**abuse the 'safe harbour' rule on the pretext of user uploads, disseminate infringing works on the pretext of fair use, and delay performance of notice-and-takedown obligations on the pretext of self-devised procedures**". Platform rules change frequently; check the current official page.

**2. Notice-and-takedown and safe harbour (China).** Under the Regulation on the Protection of the Right of Communication through Information Networks (信息网络传播权保护条例) (State Council Order No. 468, in force 2006-07-01, amended 2013): **Article 14** — the right holder may submit a written notice to a network service provider that provides information storage space or search and linking services, which must contain the right holder's name, contact details and address, the name and network address of the infringing content, and **preliminary proof of the infringement**, and the right holder is responsible for its authenticity; **Article 15** — on receiving the notice, the provider "shall **immediately delete** ... and shall at the same time forward the notice to ... the subscriber"; **Article 16** — the subscriber may submit a written statement (counter-notice) requesting restoration; **Article 17** — on receiving the written statement, the provider "shall **immediately restore** ... and the right holder may no longer notify ... deletion". **Article 22** — the five conditions for exemption in respect of information storage space **must all be satisfied** (expressly marking and publicly disclosing the information, not altering the work, "not knowing and having no reasonable grounds to know" of the infringement, not directly obtaining economic benefit, and deleting on notice); **Article 23** — search and linking services that disconnect links on notice are not liable, "**but where it knows or should know of the infringement, it shall bear joint liability for the infringement**"; **Articles 20 and 21** set out the conditions for exemption for automatic access/transmission and automatic caching; **Article 24** — the right holder compensates losses caused by wrongful deletion. China Court Network case commentary: the **precondition** for relying on the safe harbour is to prove that one is indeed a provider of information storage space or search and linking services; a qualified notice must contain ownership and infringement evidence; whether deletion was timely is **determined comprehensively** under Article 14 of the Provisions of the Supreme People's Court on Several Issues Concerning the Application of Law in the Trial of Civil Dispute Cases Involving Infringement of the Right of Communication through Information Networks (最高人民法院关于审理侵害信息网络传播权民事纠纷案件适用法律若干问题的规定), in the light of the form of the notice, its accuracy, the fame and number of the works and other factors.

**3. US DMCA §512 and the four fair use factors.** **17 U.S.C. §512** (U.S. Copyright Office) provides limitations on liability (safe harbours) for **four** categories of online service providers, three of which must comply with notice-and-takedown; a valid notice must contain six elements (the signature of the right holder, identification of the work, information locating the infringing material, contact details, a statement of good-faith belief that the use is unauthorised, and a statement of the accuracy of the notice under penalty of perjury); the service provider must remove the material expeditiously and notify the uploading user; the user may submit a counter-notification, and on receipt the service provider must restore access within **not less than 10 and not more than 14 business days**, unless the original notifier has filed an action. **17 U.S.C. §107 — the four fair use factors** (copyright.gov): ① **the purpose and character of the use** (including whether the use is commercial; "**transformative** use is more likely to be fair use" — "add something new, with a further purpose or different character, and **do not substitute for the original use of the work**"); ② **the nature of the copyrighted work** (creative works are treated less favourably than factual works); ③ **the amount and substantiality of the portion used** (even a small amount may fail where it takes the "**heart**" of the work); ④ **the effect of the use upon the potential market for or value of the work**. The Office stresses case-by-case assessment: "**there is no formula to ensure that a predetermined percentage or amount** may be used without permission". **Difference from Chinese law**: US fair use is an **open-ended general standard**, whereas Article 24 of the Chinese law is a **closed list plus a catch-all provision**; **the same video may infringe in China and be fair use in the United States, and vice versa**, and US reasoning cannot be used to justify publication in China.

**4. YouTube Content ID (official help documentation).** Content ID uses audio and video reference files submitted by right holders to generate "fingerprints" that automatically scan uploaded videos, and a match generates a claim; right holders can set three actions: **Block**, **Monetize** and **Track**. The official documentation states: "Please note that **you can specify any of these actions individually in different geographic locations** ... for the same video, the copyright owner may apply a monetization policy in one country/region but choose to block or track the video in another" — this is **the direct basis for geo-blocking of music and content**. Google's *YouTube Copyright Transparency Report* (2025 data, early-2026 snapshot) states that "rights and policies may be specific to certain countries, resulting in a video being **monetized in one country and blocked in another**", and shows that **in 2025 right holders chose "monetize" for more than 90% of claims**; an official blog post explains that fingerprints can already recognise **cropped and mirrored** content. **Key distinction**: a Content ID claim **does not generate a copyright strike**; a valid complaint submitted by a right holder through the DMCA webform results in removal and a **copyright strike**, and **three strikes within 90 days** terminates the account and associated channels, which can be resolved by expiry after 90 days, withdrawal of the request or a counter-notification; right holders who repeatedly issue erroneous claims may have their Content ID access terminated.

**5. Article 17 of the EU DSM Directive (EU) 2019/790.** Drawing on EUR-Lex, the Commission's Guidance on Article 17 (COM(2021) 288 final) and C-401/19: **17(1)(2)** — where an online content-sharing service provider (OCSSP) provides the public with protected content **uploaded by users**, this constitutes an "**act of communication to the public / making available to the public**" and requires authorisation from the right holders, and that authorisation also covers the relevant acts of users where they are not acting commercially or their activity does not generate significant revenue; **17(3)** — in that case the liability limitation in **Article 14(1) of Directive 2000/31/EC** does not apply, Article 17 being **lex specialis** and "**not creating any new rights**"; **17(4)** — absent authorisation, a provider is exempt only if it proves that it has ① **made best efforts to obtain authorisation**, ② **made best efforts to ensure** that specific works about which right holders have provided information **are unavailable**, and ③ **acted expeditiously** on a sufficiently substantiated notice to remove the content and prevent **future uploads**; **17(5)** — assessment in the light of **proportionality**; **17(6)** — a different liability regime applies to new service providers with annual turnover below EUR 10 million and to similar providers; **17(7)** — cooperation **must not result in the unavailability of non-infringing content**, and Member States must ensure that users may rely on the exceptions for **(a) quotation, criticism and review; (b) use for the purposes of caricature, parody or pastiche**; **17(8)** — "shall not lead to any **general monitoring obligation**"; **17(9)** — effective and expeditious complaint and redress mechanisms must be established, and blocking or removal decisions must be subject to **human review** with out-of-court redress available. **C-401/19 (Poland v Parliament and Council, judgment of 2022-04-26)**: the Court of Justice of the European Union **dismissed** the action for annulment, holding that the provision complies with Article 11 and the proportionality requirement of Article 52(1) of the Charter of Fundamental Rights of the European Union, while stressing that a filtering system which "cannot adequately distinguish unlawful from lawful content, leading to the blocking of lawful communication" would not comply with freedom of expression. → EU platforms tend towards **pre-upload filtering**, and parody, pastiche and quotation, although statutorily protected, may still be wrongly blocked.

**6. Cross-border territorial differences and music geo-blocking.** Copyright is **territorial**: rights are licensed country by country, and the music rights chain (composition, recording, performance, collective management) is often split by territory. Content ID can set Block / Monetize / Track separately for each country/region, so that the same video may be viewable in country A, blocked in country B and monetized in country C. **The two dimensions compound**: domestic library music being "for this platform only" plus overseas territorial licensing make "multi-platform + cross-border" risks multiplicative. In practice: check the licensed territory; prepare a **music-free version or a version with a replacement audio track** as a territorial fallback; pre-check with copyright verification tools or YouTube Studio Content Manager; keep evidence for each region.

## 2. Comparison of rules by jurisdiction

| Jurisdiction | Model of exceptions/limitations | Platform liability | Key differences |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| China | Article 24 **closed list** + three-step test | Notice-and-takedown + safe harbours in Articles 20–23 of the Regulation | No general fair use; clip-splitting and adapted compilations are specifically targeted |
| United States | 17 U.S.C. §107 **open-ended four factors** | DMCA §512 four categories of safe harbour + counter-notification within 10–14 business days | More room for transformative use, but case by case and with no safe line |
| European Union | Statutory exceptions (quotation, criticism, review, caricature/parody/pastiche) + Article 17 | OCSSPs bear "communication to the public" liability directly; pre-upload filtering; human review | High risk of wrongful blocking by automated filters; no general monitoring |
| Platform level | Each platform's user agreement and music-library licence | Content ID, copyright checks, copyright strikes | Geo-blocking; account termination after three strikes |

## 3. What this means for editing, and risk tiers

1. Before publication, list every target platform and country/region, check the licensed territory for each, and prepare a substitute audio track for territories not covered.
2. Do not use US fair use or transformative reasoning to justify publication within China (a mismatch of jurisdictions).
3. On receiving a notice, first distinguish a DMCA takedown (with a strike) from a Content ID claim (no strike), then decide between a counter-notification and a dispute.
4. A notice sent as a right holder must contain the statutory elements and preliminary proof, to avoid liability for wrongful deletion (Article 24 of the Regulation).

| Tier | Conduct | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 🔴 Extremely high | Distributing a finished video using platform A's library music to Bilibili, Xiaohongshu or overseas | "This platform only" + territorial licensing: a double breach |
| 🔴 Extremely high | Cross-border distribution of music or film/TV clips without overseas territorial licences | Territoriality + Content ID blocking/monetization |
| 🔴 Extremely high | Refusing to delete after a qualified notice; abusing the safe harbour on the ground of "user uploads" | The exemption conditions in Articles 22/23 of the Regulation are not met |
| 🟠 High | Using US fair use to justify publication in China; mistaking a claim for a DMCA takedown | Mismatch of jurisdictions; three strikes (90 days) can terminate the account |
| 🟠 High | Publishing parody/pastiche in the EU without appeal materials ready | Pre-upload filtering under Article 17 may wrongly block it |
| 🟡 Medium | Sending an unqualified notice; failing to counter-notify promptly after a wrongful deletion | The notice is ineffective; wrongful deletion requires compensation |
| 🟢 Low | The licence covers all platforms and territories, a substitute audio track is ready and pre-checks have been done | Evidence for each region must still be retained |

## 4. Cross-border publication decision flow and self-check checklist

```
Q1 Have the target publication territories (domestic + overseas) been listed one by one?
Q2 Does the licensed territory cover all target territories? No → prepare a music-free / replacement-track version or abandon that region
Q3 Cross-platform? → Platform library music must not cross platforms; switch to a multi-platform commercial licence
Q4 Is the target platform an EU OCSSP? → Expect pre-upload filtering; prepare quotation/parody explanations and appeal materials
Q5 Has a Content ID pre-check been run on the YouTube side? → Anticipate Block/Monetize/Track differences by territory
Q6 Notice received: DMCA takedown (with strike) or Content ID claim (no strike)?
   DMCA → assess a counter-notification (restoration within 10–14 business days) Content ID → dispute / withdraw / replace the audio track
Q7 Are you the right holder? → The notice must contain the six statutory elements (US) / three (China) + preliminary proof
```

- [ ] All target platforms and countries/regions have been listed, the licensed territory covers them, and a substitute audio track is ready for territories not covered
- [ ] Music from one platform's library has not been distributed across platforms; Chinese Article 24 and US fair use have been kept distinct
- [ ] It is understood that fair use has no "X seconds/X%" safe line; parody explanations and appeal materials are ready for EU publication
- [ ] DMCA takedowns (strike) can be distinguished from Content ID claims (no strike); the consequences of 3 strikes in 90 days are known
- [ ] Right-holder notices contain the statutory elements and preliminary proof; the deadlines for counter-notification/appeal (including 10–14 business days) are recorded
- [ ] Each platform's current official copyright rules, library terms and appeal channels have been checked and filed

## 6. TO VERIFY and points of contention

1. **The current official rules of each domestic platform** (copyright and derivative-edit rules, library terms and appeal procedures of Douyin, Bilibili, Kuaishou and WeChat Channels) **have not been checked against the official agreements item by item**; the three limits come from second-hand accounts by courts and copyright bodies, and the rules change frequently.
2. How the **online infringement provisions of the Civil Code** (Articles 1194–1197) fit with the Regulation has not been verified.
3. **Article 14 of the Supreme People's Court judicial interpretation on the right of communication through information networks**: the article number and the "timeliness" factors come from a China Court Network account and have not been checked against the original text.
4. The Regulation (2006/2013) has not been updated following the 2020 amendment of the Law and the Civil Code; the current approach to applying notice-and-takedown is TO VERIFY.
5. **Developments in US §107 case law**: only the U.S. Copyright Office overview has been checked; how the approach to "transformative use" changed after *Andy Warhol Foundation v. Goldsmith* (2023), and subsequent amendments to §512, have not been researched in depth.
6. **The "10–14 business days" for DMCA counter-notification** is a period under US law, whereas Article 17 of the Chinese Regulation requires "immediate restoration"; the two must not be conflated.
7. **Currency of the YouTube rules**: the transparency report cited is 2025 data / an early-2026 snapshot, and policies may change.
8. **National implementing legislation on Article 17 of the DSM Directive differs** (the definition of "pastiche" and the thresholds in Germany, France, Italy and elsewhere); the United Kingdom **does not apply** the Directive following Brexit; these have not been verified one by one.
9. **The case number and judgment date in C-401/19** (2022-04-26, from the Court's press release): the full judgment reference is TO VERIFY.
10. **Whether domestic platform music is licensed territorially, and the specific territorial restrictions on cross-border publication**: only the fact that Content ID can set policies by country/region has been verified.
11. **The detailed calculation of "three strikes in 90 days"** (whether categories are aggregated, whether the appeal period counts) rests only on YouTube's official overview.
12. **Cumulative cross-border data and personality-rights compliance**: the outbound data transfer rules of the Personal Information Protection Law (from Article 38 onwards) and the GDPR have not been verified for this edition.
